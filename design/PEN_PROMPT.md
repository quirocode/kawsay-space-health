You are designing **KAWSAY**, a tablet app for astronauts on long-duration missions. It is a prototype for NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 ("Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions"). Design the visual system and the 7 screens listed below, with real content.

## 1. Product in one sentence
KAWSAY answers the question no health dashboard answers: **"What body will I land with?"** It shows the crew member today next to their projected self on arrival day, connects symptoms the way spaceflight hazards compound each other, and closes the loop (measure → understand → act → verify → escalate) when Earth is 14–20 minutes away.

## 2. User and context of use
- **Crew member on a Mars transit**, not a physician (engineer, pilot or scientist with basic medical training).
- Uses it **under 3 minutes a day**, usually in the morning, between tasks.
- **Tablet, landscape, ~11 inch (1194 × 834 pt)**, mounted on a wall or floating loose, held in one hand. Cabin lighting is dim and warm at "night".
- May be **wearing gloves** or **floating**, so: big targets, no precise gestures, no hover, no long-press, no swipe-only actions.
- Their four questions, in this order: **Am I OK? What do I do now? Did it work? Do I need to tell Earth?**
- Secondary user: the **flight surgeon on Earth**, who reads a one-screen brief 14 minutes late.
- Judges will see the app for **10 seconds in a 30-second video**. The main screen must explain the idea without words from us.

## 3. Principles (non-negotiable)
1. **One decision per screen.** Each screen has one primary button. Everything else is secondary.
2. **Readable at a glance, from 1 m away.** The state of the body must be clear in under 2 seconds.
3. **Colour is never alone.** Every status = colour + icon + word ("OK", "Watch", "Act", "Old data").
4. **Calm before alarm.** No flashing, no sirens, no red full-screen takeovers. Red is rare and quiet. Copy is short and imperative, never scary.
5. **No sci-fi HUD.** No neon blue on black, no hexagon grids, no glowing scanlines, no fake telemetry noise, no tiny monospace everywhere. This is an instrument for a tired human, not a movie prop.
6. **Stale data looks stale.** A reading that is too old is never green: it is grey, hatched, and says "Old data · 3 days".
7. **Honest numbers.** Every figure shows a small source tag: `NASA` (official) or `Prototype` (our value). Tapping "Why?" shows the source.

## 4. Visual direction — "Warm instrument"
KAWSAY means **"life"** in Quechua, the language of the Andes. Use it with restraint: **no** Inca clichés, no fake ruins, no llamas, no ornamental "ethnic" borders. Borrow two things only:

- **Natural-dye palette.** Andean weavers dye wool with cochineal (red), q'olle (yellow), and plant greens and indigos. These pigments are earthy, warm and calm, which is exactly what a stressed person in a metal tube needs and the opposite of every other team's neon-on-black.
- **The woven line as a metaphor for time.** A textile is built one row at a time, like a mission is built one day at a time. Use a **thin horizontal woven band** (simple stepped/striped geometry, 1–2 colours, very subtle) as the mission timeline and as the track of the time slider. Each day = one "row". Missed exercise days show as thinner, lighter threads. This is our signature element; use it in 2–3 places only.

Overall feel: warm off-white "paper" background in day mode, deep warm charcoal (not blue-black) in night mode; generous whitespace; big, friendly numbers; soft shadows; rounded but not bubbly.

Explain in a short note on the cover frame why this identity was chosen (2–3 sentences).

## 6. The centrepiece — the double body (Today / Arrival day)
Two simple, gender-neutral, front-facing human figures side by side, the same size:
- **Left: "Today · Sol 142".** Solid figure in `ink`/`surface` with the 10 zones tinted by today's status.
- **Right: "Arrival · Sol 180".** The same figure drawn in `future` (Dusk), slightly translucent, like a reflection. Its zones are tinted by the **projected** status at arrival.
- Between them, a short **woven timeline** with a draggable handle (the time slider) from today to arrival. Dragging it morphs the right figure.
- **Difference between the two bodies:** a zone that gets worse by arrival shows a thin outline in the status colour plus a small delta label (e.g. "Bone −4.8 %"). Zones that are the same stay quiet. Do not use arrows all over the body; max 3 delta labels at once.
- The **10 zones** (each must be its own tappable shape/area, at least 56 pt): `eyes`, `brain`, `inner_ear`, `heart`, `lungs`, `bones` (spine + legs), `muscles` (thigh/arm), `kidneys`, `skin_immune` (skin outline / hand), `gut`.
- Zone states to draw: OK, Watch, Act, Old data (hatched), Selected (thick `brand` outline), Pressed.

Under the arrival figure: the **Landing Readiness** score (big number, e.g. **66**) with "with your plan: 89", and the **four landing-day tasks** as four cards:
1. **Stand up without fainting** (heart)
2. **Read the displays** (eyes)
3. **Climb out and walk** (bones, muscles, inner ear)
4. **Run the landing checklist** (brain)

Each card: icon, task, status word+colour now, and "with plan" status.

## 7. Screens (use this exact demo content: "Sol 142")
Mission: **Mars transit, Sol 142 of 180. One-way delay to Earth: 14 min.** Crew member: **A. Quispe** (fictional). Time: 07:40.

### 7.1 Home · Future Self (the hero screen)
- Top bar: "Sol 142 · Mars transit · Earth delay 14 min" + environment chip + small "Demo" pill.
- The double body (section 6). Today: all zones OK. Arrival: **bones** and **muscles** in **Watch** (amber).
- Readiness: **66** · "with your plan: 89". A two-option switch "Current habits / With my plan" flips the arrival figure and the tasks.
- Tasks: Stand **Watch → OK**, Read displays **OK → OK**, Climb out and walk **Watch → Watch** (bone needs more than 38 days), Checklist **Watch → OK**.
- One insight line: "6 days of incomplete exercise moved your arrival body. 38 more minutes a day brings it back."
- Primary button: **"Start today's check (2 min)"**. Secondary: "Adjust my plan".
- Small "Plan" panel (can be a sheet): exercise 52 → 90 min/day (NASA target: 1.5 h/day, `NASA` tag), water 1.6 → 2.5 L, sleep 6.1 → 7 h. Changing a value visibly moves the arrival figure and the score.

### 7.2 Daily check (under 3 minutes)
Stepper, one question per screen, big controls, progress "3 of 7".
1. Blood pressure **118/76 mmHg** (number pad, big keys)
2. Heart rate **64 bpm**
3. Temperature **36.7 °C**
4. Glucose **92 mg/dL**
5. Sleep last night **6.4 h** (slider in 0.5 h steps)
6. Mood **4 / 5** (five large faces or levels, with words)
7. Symptoms checklist: Headache · Dizziness · Blurred vision · Joint pain · Rash or cold sore · Trouble sleeping · Confusion · None (big toggles)
- Each step: "How to measure" link (≤4 short steps) and "Skip". Last step: summary "All OK today" with the 10-zone mini body.

### 7.3 Zone detail (example: Lungs)
Sheet from the right. Zone name + status. Indicators in this zone with value, mini 7-day trend, status, freshness ("2 h ago"). One example **Old data**: "SpO₂ · Old data · 3 days" (hatched). "Why?" opens the source: band ("Below 92 %: Watch"), source label, `Prototype` or `NASA` tag, link. Risks it relates to: Hypoxia, Decompression. Primary: "Measure now".

### 7.4 Finding and action (compound risk)
Later that day the crew member reports a headache. Card titled **"Headache + short sleep + high cabin CO₂"**, status **Watch**.
- "Because": Headache reported 13:10 · Sleep averaged 5.2 h for 3 nights · Cabin CO₂ 4.1 mmHg (NASA-STD-3001 limit: 3 mmHg, 1-hour average; `NASA` tag).
- Hazards involved shown as small chips: Hostile environment · Isolation.
- One action, big: **"Move to a well-ventilated module, drink 250 ml of water, and tell the crew to check the CO₂ scrubber."**
- "We'll check again in **30 min**" with a small clock. Primary: **"Done, start the timer"**.

### 7.5 Verification
30 minutes later. "Did it work?" Re-measure: headache 4/5 → **2/5**, cabin CO₂ **2.6 mmHg**. Result: **"Improved"** (OK). Two buttons: "Close" (primary) and "Send to flight surgeon". Show the alternative state too: "No better → we'll send this to Earth" (Watch).

### 7.6 Flight surgeon brief
One screen, printable feel, readable by a doctor in 30 seconds.
- Header: "Brief · Sol 142 · A. Quispe". Big line: **"Arrives on Earth in 14 min (14:02)"**, plus "Reply earliest: 14:16".
- Headline: "Headache linked to high cabin CO₂ and short sleep; improved after action."
- What changed · What the crew member did (with "Improved" outcome) · **Decision requested:** "Confirm scrubber check; any change to sleep schedule?"
- Landing readiness 66 → 89 with plan.
- Footer line: "Handled on board. A question to Earth would have taken 28 min just to get an answer."
- Primary: "Queue for downlink". Show the **conjunction** state: "No contact with Earth until Sol 158 · Brief stored".

### 7.7 Environment selector
Five cards: **ISS** (delay ~0 s), **Orion / Moon transit**, **Moon surface** (1/6 g), **Mars transit** (≤20 min, selected, 14 min now), **Mars surface** (3/8 g). Each card: gravity, one-way delay, icon. Plus a toggle "Solar conjunction — no contact". Primary: "Use this environment".
